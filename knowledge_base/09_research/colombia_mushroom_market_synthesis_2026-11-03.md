# Culinary and medicinal mushrooms in Colombia
## Research synthesis and commercial implications

**Research cutoff: 3 November 2026.** All prices are Colombian pesos (COP) unless marked otherwise. Trade values are nominal US dollars. Written for a small Colombian producer considering the Bogotá–Sabana market, with national context where evidence permits.

## Integration with Setas de la Peña governance

**Question:** what do documented Colombian offers, buyer applications, trade and product-specific evidence establish about culinary and medicinal mushroom opportunities?

**Current project position:** `DECISIONS.md` DEC-013 retains shiitake as the startup species. `07_business/pricing.md` and DEC-020 retain the approved 40% contribution-margin floor on net collected price and require measured costs before a selling price. This research changes neither decision. References to orellana or multi-species tests below are external-market hypotheses, not authorization to introduce another species.

**Agreement:** reinforces the existing distinction between asking prices and farm economics, and between food and therapeutic claims. **Conflict:** qualifies the unsupported assertions in `07_business/colombian_market.md` that industrial suppliers offer only Agaricus and that a local specialty-supply gap is established. **Novelty:** named nearby portfolios and restaurant applications, 2025 format-specific trade, and explicit regulatory/clinical transfer boundaries. See also the separately dated snapshot `docs/research/precios-setas-colombia-2026-11.md`; different sellers, packs and dates are not reconciled into a single market price.

**Evidence confidence:** high for the quoted official statistics and narrowly stated legal/study findings; commercial listings remain unverified observations, not scientific Tier 2 sources under CANON. **Local applicability:** useful for shiitake buyer research, but demand, margins, current authorizations and supplier performance require direct verification. No field measurements were collected.

**Routing:** dated consultative research, with a measurement opportunity in `09_research/active_research_knowledge.md` and open questions in `09_research/unresolved_questions.md`. Not an SOP, active price list, production plan, clinical recommendation or authorization. A source can be reliable about its own narrow result without proving local profitability.

## 1. What the evidence actually says

Colombia has an established culinary mushroom business, a visible specialty-food segment, and a commercially active but difficult-to-verify market for products sold on medicinal or functional positioning. These segments overlap in species but differ in buyers, competing products, distribution costs and regulatory requirements. Treating them as one growing “mushroom market” obscures the decisions a producer needs to make.

My assessment is that a small producer's most defensible initial commercial test is a narrowly specified fresh culinary offer to identifiable local buyers. That conclusion rests on observed menus and competing offers, rather than an assumption that Colombia has an underserved national market. It is conditional: production reliability, paid repeat orders and contribution after delivery must support it. A differentiated medicinal line could become a separate opportunity, but neither premium bottle prices nor preliminary clinical research establish its profitability.

Five findings materially shape that assessment:

1. **The local specialty market already has established competitors.** Setas Doradas lists orellanas, shiitake and eryngii alongside conventional champiñones and processed products. Cuivá lists oyster mushrooms as well as familiar retail formats. Species choice alone offers limited differentiation. [Setas Doradas catalogue](https://setasdoradas.com/productos-dorados/), [Cuivá catalogue](https://www.champinonessetasdecuiva.com/productos/).
2. **There are specific culinary uses worth targeting.** Dondoh's Bogotá menu names shiitake and eryngii. Casa Lelyte's published menu names orellana and shiitake preparations and a lion's-mane main course. These are observable applications, although their purchasing quantities remain unknown. [Dondoh menu](https://dondoh.com/bogota/carta/), [Casa Lelyte menu](https://menupp.co/casalelyte/venue/wZ4FzjNurM2RLk8CMEwl/menu/3de4c0d2-a69a-440b-87d0-ee1ba6cf7875).
3. **Prices vary substantially across offers.** A direct seller advertises orellanas at COP 22,000/kg on promotion, while Collecta lists a 250 g pack at COP 14,000, equivalent to COP 56,000/kg. These are different commercial offers, not proof of an available grower margin. [Terra Seta](https://www.terraseta.co/), [Collecta](https://collecta.com.co/producto/orellanas/).
4. **Trade trends differ by format.** In the reviewed WITS series, fresh exports declined in 2025, preserved exports expanded and dried imports contracted. A single growth narrative cannot explain all three. The tables below preserve the classifications and denominators.
5. **Medicinal positioning requires a separate evidence and authorization strategy.** An edible mushroom, a fortified food and a supplement containing the same species cannot inherit each other's classification, claims or trial evidence. [Decree 3249](https://normograma.invima.gov.co/compilacion/docs/decreto_3249_2006.htm), [Resolution 2674](https://normograma.invima.gov.co/compilacion/docs/resolucion_minsaludps_2674_2013.htm).

## 2. Scope, method and limits

This is a targeted desk-research synthesis. It combines official trade statistics, Colombian regulatory texts, official local initiatives, first-party producer and retailer catalogues, restaurant menus, selected primary human studies and analytical-method research. It is not a statistically representative consumer survey, a full customs extraction, an exhaustive competitor census or a systematic clinical review.

Sources were selected for their ability to answer concrete questions: what products are on offer, at what advertised price and pack size, where mushrooms enter food service, what trade measures show, and what authorizations or claims require closer examination. Promotional claims are treated as evidence of seller positioning only. They are not evidence of efficacy, certification, sales volume or customer retention.

The evidence labels used here are:

| Evidence class | Meaning in this report | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **Field-measured** | Paid invoices, buyer interviews, farm records or independently collected local measurements | None were collected for this report |
| **Tier 1** | Official statistics, legal texts, official program records and peer-reviewed primary research | Authority applies to the specific fact, jurisdiction or experiment, not every commercial inference |
| **Tier 2** | Recognized technical books, manufacturer specifications and national research institutions, per CANON | Technical scope and transfer limits still apply |
| **Unverified commercial observation** | Vendor offers and private financial compilations, retained as Tier 3/context-only evidence | Listing observed, but stock, transactions, sales and independent validity remain unverified |
| **Tier 3 / hypothesis** | My interpretation, proposed positioning and validation design | Requires local commercial testing |
| **Unverified** | Current stock, seller-reported registry numbers, actual transaction prices, certification validity and buyer quantities without independent confirmation | Must not be treated as established company or regulatory state |

Research coverage is strongest for Bogotá and the Sabana, where the most concrete price and menu observations were found. That makes the conclusions useful for a Tenjo-area producer, but unsuitable for extrapolating national penetration or willingness to pay. The original external desk research used no private farm records. This KB edition adds the governance reconciliation above from repository documents. No business was contacted and no order was placed.

## 3. Market structure: several businesses under one species umbrella

### 3.1 Familiar fresh mushrooms

Champiñón, crimini and portobello belong in a commercially familiar offer set. Cuivá's catalogue includes fresh formats, mixed packs and preserved products. Its website presents large retail chains and restaurant businesses among its customers. Those are supplier-reported relationships, not independently verified procurement contracts. They nevertheless show that an entrant competes with a business offering several channels and formats, rather than with isolated growers. [Cuivá website](https://www.champinonessetasdecuiva.com/).

**Commercial interpretation:** a small supplier entering familiar mushrooms must justify why a buyer should add or replace a supplier. Possible reasons include a particular specification, dependable delivery to an underserved route, useful order size or documented quality. General freshness and sustainability language is unlikely to establish that reason by itself.

### 3.2 Specialty fresh mushrooms

Orellanas, shiitake and eryngii are visible in established Colombian catalogues. Lion's mane also has a documented culinary application in Bogotá. Specialty does not necessarily mean scarce, newly introduced or commanding a uniform premium. Availability in a catalogue establishes an offer, while a menu establishes an application; neither measures the unmet need between them.

**Commercial interpretation:** the useful unit of analysis is a species–format–buyer combination. “Shiitake for a restaurant's existing dish, delivered to its specification” is testable. “Gourmet mushrooms for the Colombian market” is too broad to estimate demand or design a route.

### 3.3 Culinary products with additional processing

Snacks, dips, preserved mushrooms, dried ingredients and ready-to-use preparations already exist. Golden Bites, a Setas Doradas brand, advertises mushroom snacks and dips. This is direct evidence against treating value-added mushroom foods as an uncontested category. It does not establish the sales or economics of that brand. [Golden Bites portfolio](https://setasdoradas.com/productos-dorados/golden-bites/).

Processing can change convenience, distribution and shelf-life requirements, but it also introduces development, packaging, quality and authorization work. It should be assessed as another product business. It is not automatically a profitable destination for unsold fresh harvest.

### 3.4 Functional foods, powders, tinctures and supplements

Products using lion's-mane branding appear as foods as well as capsules or tinctures. Doctor Rojas describes a powder combining coconut, inulin and lion's mane with fortification. Farmatodo lists a Natural Freshly product as a food. These formulations cannot be compared with a pure extract by total pack weight. The public listings do not establish equivalent mushroom content. [Doctor Rojas listing](https://doctorrojas.co/products/melena-leon-x-100gr), [Farmatodo listing](https://www.farmatodo.com.co/producto/271700375-fungi-melena-de-leon-natural-freshly-tarro-x-40-porciones).

**Commercial interpretation:** “medicinal mushrooms” is a positioning category in retail conversation. A producer still has to decide whether it will sell an edible ingredient, formulated food, registered supplement, or another specifically assessed product. Each implies a different competitor set and cost structure.

### A cautious company-scale indicator

Croma, a private financial-data compiler, reproduces Setas Colombianas operating revenue of approximately COP 96.65 billion in 2023, COP 101.08 billion in 2024 and COP 114.35 billion in 2025. It says its inputs come from statements submitted to Supersociedades. The original filings were not independently retrieved in this research, so these remain **Tier 3 / unverified financial indicators**, not verified audited figures here. Croma is not the government website. [Croma company page](https://supersociedades.usecroma.com/setas-colombianas-sa-800151988).

They are useful as a lead for verifying incumbent scale. They should not become a national market estimate: company revenue can include different formats and destinations, excludes other suppliers and sits at a different point in the value chain from consumer retail expenditure. Revenue growth also does not identify volume growth, price changes or specialty-species growth.

## 5. Buyers: observable applications and unresolved procurement questions

### Restaurant demand has more concrete support than a generic “gourmet trend”

Dondoh's Bogotá menu includes a preparation explicitly using shiitake and eryngii. That matters because these mushrooms enter a named dish rather than an undifferentiated vegetarian category. It still leaves the supplier, purchasing frequency and required kilograms unknown. [Dondoh](https://dondoh.com/bogota/carta/).

Casa Lelyte's published menu includes shiitake/orellana koftas and a lion's-mane main course listed at COP 71,000. This demonstrates that lion's mane has a culinary application locally; its opportunity need not depend entirely on capsule marketing. The menu does not reveal the mushroom portion weight or the restaurant's ingredient purchase price. A finished-dish price cannot be reverse-engineered into a grower price without those inputs. [Casa Lelyte](https://menupp.co/casalelyte/venue/wZ4FzjNurM2RLk8CMEwl/menu/3de4c0d2-a69a-440b-87d0-ee1ba6cf7875).

A plant-based restaurant is not the only plausible buyer. A kitchen already using mushrooms as a side, sauce or named preparation may offer a more direct opening than a restaurant that must invent a new dish. This is a sales-prioritization hypothesis derived from the menus, not evidence that the identified restaurants want another supplier.

### Household retail has different transaction economics

Small packs allow consumers to try a specialty mushroom without buying a kilogram. However, packing, order preparation, customer acquisition and the delivery stop still have to be paid for. Basket merchants address part of that problem by combining mushrooms with other groceries and scheduling deliveries. Their model suggests a useful question for a grower: can an existing basket or specialty shop aggregate demand more cheaply than a new direct-to-consumer route?

The answer cannot be read from the advertised per-kilogram equivalent. Retailers may require a lower buy-in price, reject products, impose payment terms or absorb work that the producer would otherwise perform. No wholesale terms were collected for this report.

### Other plausible channels remain hypotheses

Hotels, caterers, meal businesses and ingredient distributors may be relevant, but their local mushroom purchasing was not quantified here. Similarly, supplying dried material to a functional-food manufacturer might avoid some consumer-brand costs, but no buyer specification or off-take commitment was found. These should remain research leads rather than additions to an assumed addressable market.

### Why this is still not a national market-size calculation

Domestic apparent consumption would require compatible domestic production, imports, exports and relevant inventory adjustments for a consistently defined product. This research did not find a validated current production denominator covering the intended culinary and medicinal segments. The very thin 2024 fresh-import observation in WITS also warrants a detailed customs check before making strong import-dependence claims. [WITS fresh imports](https://wits.worldbank.org/trade/comtrade/en/country/COL/year/2024/tradeflow/Imports/partner/ALL/product/070951).

Adding company sales, retail prices and customs values would mix valuation stages and risk double counting. Published production estimates repeated through older literature should be traced to their original year and method before entering an investment model. A more useful immediate estimate for a small producer is achievable recurring demand from reachable buyers, built from verified purchasing rather than an unsupported national CAGR.

## 8. Medicinal positioning: commercial visibility and scientific evidence

The functional offer is commercially visible. Foods, capsules, tinctures and powders appear across the reviewed Colombian pages. That observation is stronger than saying the market is hypothetical, but weaker than saying its size or growth has been measured. Listings and social promotion do not reveal repeat purchases, customer acquisition cost or retention.

The distinction between scientific interest and a transferable product claim is particularly important here.

### Lion's mane: preliminary human findings, preparation-specific results

Docherty and colleagues' 2023 double-blind randomized pilot studied 41 healthy adults over acute and 28-day assessments. It reported improvement in an acute Stroop measure and a day-28 stress trend at **p = 0.051**, alongside null and some negative findings. A trend at that p-value should not be rewritten as an established stress-reduction effect. [Primary study, DOI 10.3390/nu15224842](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38004235/).

A 2025 randomized crossover study in 18 healthy adults tested a particular fruiting-body extract acutely. Overall cognition and mood composites did not improve significantly, while one pegboard measure did. The sample, assessment timing and preparation constrain extrapolation. [Surendran et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12018234/).

These studies justify continued research. They do not establish broad cognitive enhancement across consumers, prevention of dementia, or equivalent effects from any locally sold powder or tincture. The conclusion is limited evidence for specific outcomes with specific preparations, not that every preparation has been proved ineffective.

### Reishi and turkey tail: ingredient and product identity matter

The US National Cancer Institute distinguishes research on specified preparations, including PSK associated with turkey tail, from the broader category of consumer mushroom products. That distinction matters commercially: evidence about a defined preparation used with conventional treatment cannot simply transfer to a generic tincture, and an overseas treatment context does not authorize Colombian claims. [NCI medicinal-mushroom summary](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/cam/patient/mushrooms-pdq).

There is insufficient evidence in this review to rank Colombian reishi, turkey-tail or Cordyceps products by efficacy. Nor was a Colombia-specific clinical validation found for any listed SKU. The presence of a species in a study or in traditional use is not a finished-product quality assessment.

### Quality can be specified more rigorously than marketing language

A functional-product assessment should ask what species and material are present, how the product was manufactured, what the serving contains and how composition and contaminants were tested. Terms such as “pure,” “dual extract” or a concentration ratio do not answer all those questions.

Analytical methods distinguish beta-glucans from total glucans and alpha-glucans. A 2016 primary methods paper describes this distinction. A 2026 methods study shows that modern formulation matrices can affect the indirect measurement, making assay choice and matrix suitability relevant to interpreting a certificate. These are composition measurements, not direct tests of clinical benefit. [McCleary and Draga, 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26957216/), [Sweeney et al., 2026](https://academic.oup.com/jaoac/advance-article/doi/10.1093/jaoacint/qsag041/8687117).

A defensible buying specification therefore needs identified material, declared carriers, a suitable assay and batch traceability. It should not assume every fruiting-body product is superior to every mycelial preparation. Tissue category alone does not establish equivalence to a studied preparation or the quality of a finished batch.

## 9. Colombia's regulatory distinctions affect the business model

This section interprets the cited framework for market analysis. It does not determine the classification or authorization of a proposed product.

### Fresh culinary mushrooms

Article 37 of Resolution 2674, as amended, provides exemptions from product authorization for natural foods without transformation and for foods or raw materials used exclusively by industry and gastronomy. A fresh, whole, untransformed mushroom may fall within the natural-food exemption, but that is a product-specific assessment. Exemption from an NSA, PSA or RSA does not eliminate sanitation or food-handling obligations. [Consolidated Article 37](https://normograma.invima.gov.co/compilacion/docs/resolucion_minsaludps_2674_2013.htm).

### Drying, milling and prepared foods

Resolution 719's annex explicitly distinguishes mushrooms by format: fresh/refrigerated/frozen unprocessed mushrooms appear under category 4.6.1, while dried/dehydrated mushrooms appear under 4.8.1. The risk classification and applicable authorization exemptions must be read together. A powder, spread or preserved product should not automatically inherit the treatment of a whole dried mushroom. [Resolution 719 technical annex](https://normograma.invima.gov.co/compilacion/docs/resolucion_minsaludps_0719_2015.htm).

The practical implication is that a plan to dehydrate surplus needs process, destination, shelf-life, labeling and authorization assessment. Changing the physical format can change what must be established before sale.

### Supplements and therapeutic language

Decree 3249 Article 9 requires registration for supplements. Articles 24–25 govern advertising and prohibit preventive or therapeutic claims and unsupported disease claims. A capsule or dropper bottle does not independently settle classification; ingredients, intended use and presentation remain relevant. [Decree 3249](https://normograma.invima.gov.co/compilacion/docs/decreto_3249_2006.htm).

There is a narrower, affirmative regulatory signal that should not be overlooked. INVIMA's July 2024 accepted-declarations list includes conditioned immune-support wording for Ganoderma lucidum beta-glucans and Pleurotus ostreatus beta-glucans. The Ganoderma entry refers to at least 350 mg of **beta-glucans**, accompanied by diet and exercise conditions. It is not a blanket approval for every reishi product or a statement about 350 mg of mushroom powder. [INVIMA accepted declarations, p. 5](https://www.invima.gov.co/biblioteca/download/89981).

### Enforcement and seller-reported authorization

INVIMA's May 2024 alert addresses specified extracts offered through bemascolombia.com without registration. Its 2026 Aurum alert addresses named unregistered products. These are direct evidence of enforcement against specific offers, not blanket species bans. [2024 alert](https://www.invima.gov.co/alertas-sanitarias/8763/pdf), [2026 alert](https://www.invima.gov.co/alertas-sanitarias/melena-de-leon).

Two reviewed commercial pages display authorization numbers: Doctor Rojas lists RSA-0032611-2024 and Farmatodo lists PSA-1145-2025. These are **seller-reported identifiers, not independently verified registry results** in this research. Their presence does not establish that every claim in the listing is authorized. Absence of a number in another page extract likewise does not establish an offense. The official consultation system is the appropriate next verification point. [INVIMA registry consultation](https://www.invima.gov.co/consulta-registros-sanitarios).

Psychedelic products are excluded from this opportunity assessment. They should not be folded into food or supplement demand merely because sellers group them with other mushrooms.

## 10. Where a small producer might create value

The following priorities are **Tier 3 commercial judgments**, conditional on local operating capability. They are not measured returns or instructions to invest in new capacity.

| Candidate business | Evidence in its favor | Main unresolved constraint | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fresh specialty mushrooms for existing restaurant dishes | Named menu applications and regional supply offers | Actual weekly purchasing, specifications, delivered margin and supplier switching | Strong candidate for a bounded paid test |
| Fresh lion's mane for selected culinary buyers | Public retail offer and a named Bogotá menu application | Few observed buyers and unknown repeat volume | Small, pre-sold trial rather than a core volume assumption |
| Small-pack household sales | Visible basket retail and specialty price ladders | Acquisition, packing, route density and household reorders | Test through an existing channel or concentrated route |
| Dried culinary ingredients | Existing imports and competing product portfolios | Buyer specification, landed competition and processing economics | Worth buyer-led investigation |
| Mushroom snacks, dips or preserves | Existing branded products and a distinct preserved trade trajectory | Product development, authorization, distribution and competitive cost | Separate product business, not a default outlet |
| Branded medicinal/functional line | Visible foods, capsules and tinctures | Product classification, testing, claims, customer retention and manufacturing cost | Research separately before launch commitments |

The most plausible differentiation hypothesis is **a documented service advantage around a specific product**, not merely local origin or an unusual species. Relevant offers might specify usable size, presentation, harvest/packing traceability, order cutoff, delivery window and response to a rejected batch. Whether these attributes earn a premium must be tested with actual buyers.

The freshness argument also needs discipline. A short geographic distance may help, but nearby established competitors have that possibility too. The advantage must appear in buyer experience and measured cost, rather than in a farm's location alone.

## 11. Economic questions the market research cannot answer for a farm

A species with a high shelf price can still be a poor commercial choice. The relevant calculation is contribution from accepted, paid product:

**Collected sales, after discounts and credits**

**minus variable cultivation and harvest costs**

**minus grading, packing, handling and payment costs**

**minus delivery, selling costs, rejected and unsold product costs.**

This contribution must then support fixed costs and replacement of equipment. Labor should be costed even when performed by an owner. Costs incurred on product that never sells must remain in the calculation.

For restaurants, useful questions concern usable product after trimming, performance in the intended recipe, dependable availability and payment. For household sales, order size, delivery density, acquisition and repeat purchase become particularly important. For processed or functional products, add manufacturing, testing, packaging, authorization work and inventory risk.

None of the public evidence reviewed supplies a reliable all-in cost structure for a Tenjo farm. It would therefore be premature to rank orellana, shiitake and lion's mane by expected profit or to convert a retail price into a cultivation investment return.

### A bottom-up demand scenario, explicitly illustrative

Suppose a reachable group of buyers actually placed recurring orders totaling 30 kg per week. At an assumed realized sale price of COP 35,000/kg, four weeks would generate COP 4.2 million in sales before costs. **Neither the 30 kg demand nor the price is a research finding.** The example illustrates how little a national-market narrative says about an individual route's revenue.

A defensible forecast would replace those assumptions with confirmed buyers, paid order history, actual prices, seasonal or menu changes, losses and collection dates. Capacity should be compared with those commitments rather than with the theoretical size of all Bogotá restaurants.

## 12. The next evidence collection should resolve commercial uncertainty

The main remaining gap is primary buyer and transaction evidence. Another generic market report will not resolve it.

A bounded validation study could begin with a deliberately varied group of restaurants already using mushrooms, a few specialty retailers or basket operators, and any serious ingredient buyers. This would be a purposive commercial sample, not a representative national demand survey.

For each buyer, record the actual species and format purchased, weekly quantities, current delivered price, minimum order, payment terms, acceptable substitutions, rejection reasons and the condition that would justify trying another supplier. Request supporting invoices or specifications where the buyer is willing to share them. Interest without a purchase should be recorded separately from a paid trial.

Follow a trial across multiple reorder opportunities. Measure collected revenue, accepted kilograms, credits, delivered cost, time spent selling and packing, and what happens when a crop is unavailable. A successful first sample is not evidence of an enduring account. A proposed route should also remain viable if its largest buyer pauses orders.

For medicinal products, the first evidence package should be different: a product-classification assessment, verification of the applicable authorization route, proposed label and claims review, manufacturing arrangement, composition specification, suitable testing plan and a credible estimate of the cost of acquiring and retaining customers. Scientific background alone cannot substitute for that package.

## 13. Conclusions and confidence

**High confidence:** culinary applications and multiple competing specialty offers exist in the reviewed Bogotá–Sabana evidence. Colombian regulation distinguishes products and claims. Reviewed WITS categories show different trade trajectories. These are specific, observable conclusions supported by the cited sources.

**Moderate confidence:** a focused fresh culinary offer is a sensible first commercial test for a small local producer able to serve it reliably. That is an inference from buyer applications and the ability to test a bounded offer, not a verified statement about this farm's readiness or expected profit.

**Low confidence / unresolved:** the current national size and CAGR of specialty or medicinal mushrooms, the share of unmet demand, current wholesale prices, independent validity of seller-reported registrations, and species-level profitability for a particular farm. Those require more granular statistics, registry results or field evidence.

The strongest opportunity statement the research supports is specific: **there are identifiable local uses and competing offers from which to build a paid, buyer-led market test.** For medicinal products, a differentiated quality proposition is conceivable, but commercial visibility is ahead of what this evidence can establish about product equivalence, authorized claims and durable profitability.

## Appendix A. Reproducibility and exclusions

- Trade percentage change = (new value / prior value − 1) × 100. Destination share uses the stated year's reported total. Ratios use unrounded inputs; displayed results round to one decimal unless indicated.
- Trade unit values divide reported dollars by reported quantity. They are aggregate customs ratios, not verified farm-gate prices or matched-product price indices.
- Fresh, dried and preserved quantities are not added or converted into a fresh-equivalent market. No generic drying conversion or extraction yield is assumed.
- Price observations preserve species, format and declared pack. No missing pack weight is inferred. Shipping and checkout availability were not verified.
- The report does not quantify certified-organic supply. A vendor's organic or sustainable description is not independent certification verification.
- Global or Latin American forecasts are not allocated to Colombia. Research capacity, species diversity and social-media presence are not converted into sales.
- Foreign functional-mushroom sellers appearing in Spanish-language search results were excluded when Colombian relevance or currency could not be established.
- Clinical studies were selected to examine prominent claims, not to establish treatment recommendations. Neither biological cultivation settings nor farm interventions are proposed.
- All recommended tests are proposals. No interviews, transactions, laboratory tests or field measurements were conducted for this synthesis.
